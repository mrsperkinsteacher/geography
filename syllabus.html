Stage 4

Years 7-8 · Geography 7-10 Syllabus (2024)

Stage 4

Stage 4 is where you learn to think like a geographer. Four focus areas take you from landscapes and water to the places people live and the links that connect us all.

What you'll study

Landscapes and landforms

How landscapes form, why they matter to people and how we protect them.

Liveability of places

What makes a place good to live in, and why people see places differently.

Water in the world

Where water is, who uses it, and how we share and look after it.

Interconnections and trade

How people, places and the things we buy connect us to the world.

What's expected of you

  1. 1Bring your curiosity - ask why things are where they are
  2. 2Read maps, graphs and photos as evidence
  3. 3Use geography words correctly - place, space, environment, interconnection, scale, sustainability and change
  4. 4Write answers that describe and explain, not just list
  5. 5Join in fieldwork and record what you see
  6. 6Hand in tasks on time, and ask for help early

How you'll be assessed

Task type

Skills tasks

Maps, graphs, photos and data - showing you can read and use them

Task type

Research or inquiry

Investigating a geographical question and sharing what you found

Task type

Fieldwork

Collecting and recording information outside the classroom

Task type

Topic tests and exams

Showing what you know and can explain

Fieldwork: About 10 hours of fieldwork across Stage 4.

Stage 5

Years 9-10 · Geography 7-10 Syllabus (2024)

Stage 5

Stage 5 takes your Stage 4 ideas further - biomes and food, changing places, environmental change and human wellbeing - with more evidence, more data and more of your own thinking.

What you'll study

Biomes and sustainable agriculture

The world's biomes, how they grow our food, and how to farm sustainably.

Changing places

How towns and cities grow and change, and what that means for people.

Environmental change and management

How environments change, why it matters and how we manage them.

Human wellbeing

Why wellbeing differs around the world, and what can be done about it.

What's expected of you

  1. 1Use real case studies - named places, real data, real dates
  2. 2Explain and analyse - say why and how, not just what
  3. 3Back up your points with statistics and sources
  4. 4Connect each topic to the Sustainable Development Goals
  5. 5Take part in fieldwork and use what you collect
  6. 6Plan your time across longer tasks

How you'll be assessed

Task type

Geographical inquiry

Research and a written response on a real geographical issue

Task type

Skills and data

Maps, graphs, statistics and spatial tools

Task type

Field investigation

Collecting and analysing data in a real environment

Task type

Examinations

Testing knowledge, understanding and geographical thinking

Fieldwork: About 10 hours of fieldwork across Stage 5.

Stage 5 Elective

Years 9-10 - an extra choice · Geography Elective 7-10 Syllabus (2025)

Stage 5 Elective

This Elective gives you the chance to go deeper into Geography than the mandatory course allows. You'll investigate real environments, real conflicts and real people - and develop the skills to think and write like a geographer. It's challenging, relevant and genuinely interesting.

What you'll study

Extreme environments

Deserts, ice, high mountains - the toughest places on Earth and how life copes.

Geopolitics

Power, borders and how geography shapes the decisions nations make.

Global citizenship

Your place in the world and how people act for a fairer future.

Oceanography

How oceans work, why they matter and the pressures they face.

Australia's neighbours

The places and people of our region and how we are connected.

Primary production

Farming, fishing and forestry - how the world feeds and supplies itself.

What's expected of you

  1. 1Engage seriously with each unit - read, question, and bring your own examples to class discussions
  2. 2Develop geographical skills: map reading, data interpretation, fieldwork observation and spatial analysis
  3. 3Write responses that describe, explain and evaluate - not just list facts
  4. 4Connect each unit to the UN Sustainable Development Goals and real-world case studies
  5. 5Complete all assessment tasks on time and to the best of your ability
  6. 6Think independently - the best geographers bring ideas nobody else has thought of

How you'll be assessed

Task type

Geographical inquiry

Research and written response investigating a real geographical issue

Task type

Skills assessment

Map work, data analysis and spatial skills tasks throughout the course

Task type

Examination

Written exam testing knowledge, understanding and geographical thinking

Task type

Field investigation

Observation and data collection in a real environment outside the classroom

Fieldwork: About 10 hours of fieldwork each stage, on top of the mandatory course fieldwork.

Year 11

Stage 6 - Preliminary · Geography 11-12 Syllabus (2022)

Year 11

Year 11 builds the foundation for your HSC year. You study four focus areas - how Earth's natural systems work, how people shape places, how people and environments interact, and your own Geographical Investigation. This year is about depth of thinking, not breadth of content.

What you'll study

40 hours

Earth's Natural Systems

How the Earth works as an integrated physical system - landscapes, cycles and the natural processes that shape land and water.

40 hours

People, Patterns and Processes

How humans have transformed the Earth's surface, and what makes one place different from another.

Your class will focus on one chosen study within this topic.

20 hours

Human-Environment Interactions

How natural systems and human activity interact - land cover change, climate change and the long-term consequences of how we have used the Earth.

Your class will focus on one chosen study: a geographic region, a contemporary hazard or climate change.

20 hours

Geographical Investigation

Your own geographical inquiry - you choose the question, collect data in the field, and present your findings.

What's expected of you

  1. 1Develop sophisticated geographical skills - including spatial technologies, fieldwork and data analysis
  2. 2Build extended response writing skills that explain processes, analyse patterns and evaluate strategies
  3. 3Use specific, well-chosen case studies to support every argument you make
  4. 4Engage with both physical and human geography - Year 11 covers both
  5. 5Manage your time and study independently - Year 11 requires self-discipline as well as ability
  6. 6Plan your Geographical Investigation early - choose a question you genuinely want to answer

How you'll be assessed

Task type

Fieldwork investigation

Practical data collection and analysis from a real field study site

Task type

Research task

In-depth investigation of a geographical issue using multiple sources

Task type

Examinations

Exams testing the Year 11 focus areas

Task type

Spatial technologies

GIS and mapping tasks showing your ability to interpret spatial data

How your mark is made up

40%

Knowledge and understanding

Your understanding of geographical content across all four focus areas

20%

Geographical skills and tools

Maps, graphs, data, fieldwork instruments and spatial technologies

20%

Inquiry and research

Including the Geographical Investigation and fieldwork

20%

Communication

How clearly and accurately you communicate geographical ideas in appropriate forms

Year 11 is entirely school-based - there is no external exam at the end of Year 11. Your school sets at least three formal tasks across the year.

Fieldwork: 12 hours of fieldwork are mandatory in Year 11, spread across the course.

Year 12

Stage 6 - HSC · Geography 11-12 Syllabus (2022)

Year 12

HSC Geography is where everything comes together. You study three focus areas - Global Sustainability, Rural and Urban Places, and Ecosystems and Global Biodiversity. Year 11 content is assumed knowledge. The HSC exam is 3 hours and rewards students who think clearly, write precisely and use evidence well.

What you'll study

30 hours

Global Sustainability

What sustainability means in practice, and whether a real global economic activity is actually achieving it.

Your class will study one global economic activity and evaluate its sustainability.

45 hours

Rural and Urban Places

How settlements work and change - from small rural towns to megacities - and the forces reshaping places globally.

Your class will study one rural place, one urban place and one large city outside Australia.

45 hours

Ecosystems and Global Biodiversity

How ecosystems function, what they are worth and what is happening to global biodiversity.

Your class will study two ecosystem types, with at least one from outside Australia.

What's expected of you

  1. 1Master extended response writing - HSC Geography rewards sustained, structured analytical writing
  2. 2Know your case studies inside out - specific details, locations, dates and outcomes
  3. 3Use your fieldwork - one school-based task must be based on a fieldwork activity
  4. 4Practise past HSC questions regularly - the exam format rewards familiarity and technique
  5. 5Be able to evaluate - not just describe or explain, but judge the effectiveness and significance of geographical processes and strategies
  6. 6Show up for every lesson - Year 12 Geography moves fast and every class builds on the last

How you'll be assessed

Task type

School-based tasks

Set by your school through the year, including a task based on fieldwork

Task type

Trial HSC exam

Full 3-hour exam under HSC conditions - essential preparation for October

Task type

HSC examination

3-hour written exam in October - four sections covering multiple choice, short answer and extended responses

How your mark is made up

40%

Knowledge and understanding

Across all three Year 12 focus areas

20%

Skills and tools

Maps, graphs, GIS, fieldwork instruments and data

20%

Inquiry and research

Including the mandatory fieldwork-based task

20%

Communication

Communicating geographical understanding in appropriate forms

Your school assessment mark (50%) and your HSC exam mark (50%) combine to make your final HSC mark.

The HSC exam at a glance

100 marks · 3 hours

Section I15 marks

Multiple choice

15 questions using stimulus material. About 30 minutes - bring a 2B pencil.

Section II45 marks

Short answer

4 to 6 short-answer questions, some with multiple parts. About 1 hour 20 minutes.

Section III20 marks

Structured extended response

On either Rural and Urban Places or Ecosystems and Global Biodiversity. About 35 minutes.

Section IV20 marks

Extended response

On whichever of those two topics was not in Section III. About 35 minutes.

Global Sustainability is examined in Sections I and II. Sections III and IV cover Rural and Urban Places and Ecosystems and Global Biodiversity - one each, but you will not know which is which until you open the paper. Total: 100 marks in 3 hours.

Fieldwork: 12 hours of fieldwork are mandatory in Year 12. One school-based task must be based on a fieldwork activity.
